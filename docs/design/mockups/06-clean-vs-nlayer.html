Clean Architecture, translated from n-layer

Same MetroDisplay components in both diagrams. Only one thing actually changes mechanically — everything else is vocabulary. Find that one thing and the rest stops being mysterious.

WHAT YOU KNOW — n-layer

A stack. Dependencies point down. DAL is the foundation.

CLEAN ARCHITECTURE

Rings. Dependencies point inward. DAL is on the outside.

The one mechanical difference. In n-layer, Business has a project reference to DataAccess. In Clean, that arrow is flipped: Business declares IArtifactRepository and DataAccess references Business to implement it. Nothing else in the diagram is enforced by the compiler. Everything else — ring names, four layers instead of three, "entities" vs "domain models" — is vocabulary.

Why that flip is the whole point: flow ≠ dependency

The confusion almost everyone hits coming from n-layer: at runtime the call still goes Business → database. It's the compile-time reference that reverses, not the call.

Does MetroDisplay need it?

A

n-layer naming + invert only the repository seam — recommended

Keep the three familiar layer names. Flip exactly one arrow: IArtifactRepository declared in Business. That buys the disk→blob swap and I/O-free Business tests, at the cost of one interface.

B

Full Clean, four rings

Entities separated from use cases, boundary models distinct from domain models, mapping at every crossing. Pays off on large teams and long-lived domains; noticeable ceremony for a solo display app.

C

Straight n-layer, no inversion

Business references DataAccess directly. Simplest, most familiar, and fine — but the deferred blob-storage item in §15 stops being free, and Business tests need real or faked file I/O.
